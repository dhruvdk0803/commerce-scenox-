Product photography is the cost that scales worst. A hundred products means a studio booking, a stylist and a week of editing, and the next season starts the whole thing again. This service is for stores that have photos, perhaps decent supplier shots or a quick phone shoot, and need them to look like a consistent catalog.

## What you get

- **Studio sets:** consistent backdrops, lighting and shadow across the range, so a collection page looks like one shoot.
- **White-background mains:** clean, centred product images sized for marketplaces and Merchant Center.
- **Lifestyle scenes:** the product placed in a believable setting, in styles agreed with you in advance.
- **Consistent crops and sizes:** square, portrait and zoom-ready versions for store, ads and feeds.
- **Naming and alt text:** files named by SKU, ready to match to your product data.
- **A review log:** what we changed, what we rejected and why.

## How it works

1. You send your existing product photos and your product sheet, so we know what each item is.
2. We inspect a sample, tell you what is achievable, and quote a fixed price per batch of 50 products.
3. Our AI engine produces the images. A person then compares each with your source photo, checking shape, colour, text, logos, stitching, finish and scale.
4. Images that drift from the real product are redone or dropped. We do not pass them through.
5. You approve a first batch. We then deliver the rest, and can attach the images to products in your store if you want upload handled too.

## Why accuracy comes first

AI imagery is only useful if the customer receives what the picture shows. The failure modes are well known: a bag with an extra strap, a shoe with the wrong sole, printed text that is garbled, a jewellery stone that changes colour. Returns follow, and some marketplaces treat misleading images as a policy matter.

| Risk | What the human check looks for |
| --- | --- |
| Shape and proportion | Compared against your original photo, side by side |
| Colour | Matched to the product, adjusted for lighting differences |
| Logos and printed text | Must be legible and correct, or the image is rejected |
| Fine details | Zips, buttons, stitching, ports, stones, hardware |
| Scale | The product should not look larger or smaller than it is |
| Scene plausibility | Hands, surfaces and shadows behave as they would in real life |

We would rather send you eighty accurate images out of a hundred, with a note on the twenty we could not do well, than a hundred that look good and mislead.

Different channels need different outputs. Marketplaces want a clean white-background main. Your store benefits from lifestyle scenes and detail shots. Ads want consistent crops. ## Is this a fit?

It suits stores with a catalog of 50 products or more, existing photos of reasonable quality, and a need for consistency across them. It suits teams that sell on marketplaces and need mains quickly.

It does not suit a brand whose identity depends on original editorial photography, or products where fine detail is the whole sale, such as bespoke jewellery. Real photography is better there, and we will say so. If your source photos are blurry or heavily cropped, fix those first.
