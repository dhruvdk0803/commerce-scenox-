## What electronics catalogs need

### Models that look alike

A laptop line can have a dozen near-identical models that differ by processor, memory and storage. We decide which of these are variants and which are separate products. As a rule, a choice the customer makes at the same price tier is a variant; a different model number is a separate product. Mixing the two breaks warranty and returns later.

### Units and consistency

Suppliers write "16GB", "16 GB" and "16 Gb". We normalise units, so filters work and a megabit is never confused with a megabyte. Screen sizes, capacities, wattage and dimensions follow one format throughout.

### Identifiers

Product feeds rely on brand, MPN and GTIN. Many electronics have all three; accessories from small makers often lack a GTIN. We keep identifiers apart from SKUs, as they serve different purposes. See [GTIN, MPN and brand](/guides/gtin-mpn-brand).

### Compatibility

A charger fits some phones and not others. We structure compatibility as a list of models rather than prose, so it can be reused for a filter or a "works with" block. We only record compatibility from a source you or the manufacturer give.

### Batteries and shipping

Products with lithium batteries are subject to carrier rules, and carriers commonly ask for battery type and watt-hour rating. We capture battery chemistry, capacity, whether the battery is built in or packed with the product, and package weight and dimensions. Classification is for you and your carrier to confirm.

It fits resellers, distributors and brands whose product data lives in manufacturer files and needs normalising. It also helps stores that suffer from inconsistent specs between listings.

It is less suited to a store with a handful of products you know well, or a business that needs real-time stock and price sync from a distributor, which is an integration job rather than a catalog build.
