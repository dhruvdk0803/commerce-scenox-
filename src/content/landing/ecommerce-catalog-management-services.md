Most catalog problems are not technology problems. They are a supplier sheet with four spellings of the same brand, variants hanging off the wrong parent, titles written by five different people and a category tree nobody has touched since launch. Software can store that mess very efficiently. It cannot fix it.

Ecommerce catalog management services mean someone else takes on that work. Scenox Commerce does it as a managed service: our AI engine handles the volume, a person reviews every output, and you approve before changes go live.

## What you get

A scoped catalog engagement usually covers some combination of the following:

- Title, brand and SKU normalisation to one pattern across the catalog
- Attribute clean-up: colour, size, material, compatibility, units, all in consistent values
- Variant and option structure rebuilt so parents and children match what shoppers see
- Category and collection mapping against a taxonomy you approve
- Duplicate detection and merge recommendations, never silent deletions
- Missing-field fill from your own specs and supplier files
- Description rewrites where they are thin or copied from suppliers
- A QA report listing every change, every flag and every product we could not resolve

## How it works

1. You send a store export or supplier files and tell us the platform. We never ask for a password.
2. We inspect the data and send a fixed quote, a scope list and a delivery date, in writing.
3. Our AI engine processes the catalog against your rules; an editor reviews every output and resolves the edge cases.
4. You review changes in a draft state or a staging file, with a change log, and mark corrections.
5. We apply the approved changes through a staff account, then hand over the QA report. On the monthly plan, steps 3 to 5 repeat on your schedule.

## A service, not software

Catalog tools come in two broad types. PIM systems give you a central database, workflows and integrations, and they reward teams with someone to configure and run them. Marketplace feed tools reshape data for each channel but assume the source data is already sound.

A managed service sits differently. You are buying finished data, not a place to store it. For a store with a few hundred to a few thousand products and no data team, that is usually the cheaper route. For a large operation with many suppliers and in-house staff, a PIM may be the right long-term answer, and a clean-up beforehand makes the migration far less painful. We compare the two in detail in [catalog management service vs PIM](/guides/catalog-management-service-vs-pim).

## Where catalogs usually break

Across supplier files, the same faults come up again and again. Colour values in free text ("navy", "Navy Blue", "NAVY/WHITE") that break filters. Size fields that mix units. Variants that share a SKU. Brand names that differ by capital letter and so split into two filter entries. Descriptions pasted from a manufacturer site, which also appear on twenty competitor stores. Each one is small, but at 2,000 products they compound into poor search, weak filters, rejected feed items and a team that cannot trust its own numbers.

Fixing these properly means deciding the rules first, then applying them consistently. That is the unglamorous part of the work, and it is why attribute design matters; the [product attributes and taxonomy guide](/guides/product-attributes-taxonomy) explains how we think about it.

## What stays yours, and what we report

You keep ownership of everything. Changes are made in your store or your files, never in a system you would have to leave later, and the QA report and change log are yours to keep. Each monthly cycle ends with a short summary: what was added, what was corrected, what is waiting on a decision from you and which products had gaps we could not fill from the data supplied.

Catalog work also touches more than listings. Inventory counts, price lists and images change as often as copy does, so a monthly scope can include price and stock corrections from your supplier sheets, image naming and alt text fixes, and a quarterly audit of orphaned, duplicate or out-of-stock products. Tell us which channels you sell on, and we keep the same rules across your store and any marketplace or feed that reads from it.
