"Shopify management" means very different things depending on who is selling it. One company means theme design. Another means paid ads. A third means catalog and channel operations. Most bad hires start with a mismatch between what the buyer pictured and what the provider actually does.

## Start with your own list

Before talking to anyone, write down what you need done each month. Use plain verbs: upload 80 new products, update prices from a supplier file, fix feed warnings, keep email flows current, change the homepage. Then mark each item as recurring or one-off, and note the ones only a specialist can do (design, development, ad buying). Providers differ most on this scope, so your list is the test they will be measured against.

## The 8 criteria

### 1. Scope

Ask for a written list of what is included and what is not. A good provider can state their boundaries in a few lines. Be wary of "everything ecommerce" with no exclusions: nobody does theme development, ad buying, catalog work and support staffing equally well for one low price.

### 2. Pricing transparency

You should see a fixed price or a published rate card before work starts. Check how the price scales: per product, per task, per hour or flat per month. Ask what triggers extra charges and whether tools, apps or stock imagery are billed separately.

### 3. Who does the work

Ask who actually touches your store, and who checks their work. A sales contact is not the team. Find out whether the work is done by staff, subcontractors, software or a mix, and what review step exists before changes go live. "Our experts handle it" is not an answer.

### 4. Access and security

The provider should work through a staff or collaborator account with permissions you set and can remove. Asking for the store owner's password is a red flag, full stop. Ask what level of access they need, and whether it can be narrowed. Also ask how they store your files and how long they keep them.

### 5. Reporting

Ask what you receive each month and in what form. Useful reporting lists what was changed, what was flagged for your decision and what is still open. A dashboard screenshot with no detail does not count.

### 6. Notice period and contract

Look for the minimum term, the notice period, the renewal terms and what happens to work in progress. Monthly with short notice is easiest to reason about. If there is a lock-in, you should have seen real work before agreeing to it.

### 7. Data ownership

Your product data, customer data and the work product are yours. Confirm that in writing, along with deletion on request, return of files when you leave, and whether your data is used to train third-party models.

### 8. A trial or sample

The strongest signal available is a small piece of real work on your own store or files, before a long commitment. A sample reveals quality, communication and how a provider handles your awkward cases. A portfolio shows only what they chose to show.

## Red flags

- A request for your store password or full-owner credentials
- No sample or trial on your own products, or only on products they pick
- A price that changes after you have sent your files, with no written scope
- A long minimum term with no preview of the work
- Promises of rankings, traffic or revenue they do not control
- Vague answers about who does the work, or no named review step
- Proof that is hard to verify: unnamed "clients", round numbers, reviews with no source
- Pressure to pay in full before seeing any output
- No clear way to remove access or retrieve your data

## Questions to ask on the first call

1. What exactly is included each month, and what is not?
2. Who does the work, and who reviews it before it goes live?
3. How do you access the store, and how do we remove that access?
4. What do we receive each month as a report?
5. Can you do a small sample on our products, and by when?
6. What is the minimum term and the notice period?
7. Who owns our data and the work you produce, and what happens when we leave?
8. What would you flag as out of scope for our store?
9. How do you handle missing or conflicting information in our files?
10. How quickly do you reply, and in which time zone?
