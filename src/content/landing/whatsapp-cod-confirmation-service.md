For many Indian D2C stores, cash on delivery is a large share of orders and a large share of returns. A COD parcel that is refused at the door costs shipping both ways and a unit of stock stuck in transit. Confirming the order on WhatsApp before dispatch is a cheap, familiar step, and this service builds it properly.

## What you get

- A COD confirmation flow: order placed, WhatsApp message sent with items and total, buyer taps confirm or cancel.
- Template wording prepared for WhatsApp approval, in English, Hindi or a regional language you choose.
- Order tags and status updates in your store, so your team sees confirmed, unconfirmed and cancelled orders.
- Reminder and timeout rules, with an escalation path for unresponsive buyers.
- Address confirmation or correction in the same chat, to cut failed deliveries from bad addresses.
- Dispatch and delivery updates after confirmation, if you want them.
- A handover note and a simple report for measuring confirmation rate and RTO.

## How it works

1. You share your store platform, COD volume, current RTO rate and the WhatsApp provider you use, if any.
2. We review your setup and send a fixed quote for the setup.
3. You add us as a staff or collaborator account and a provider user. No passwords are shared.
4. Our AI engine drafts templates and the flow logic; a person reviews every message, tests each branch with real orders and checks the tagging.
5. You approve the messages, we switch the flow on, and hand over the documentation. The [COD confirmation guide](/guides/whatsapp-cod-confirmation) describes the same method if you prefer to build it yourself.

## India-specific details that matter

This is a market where small details decide whether the flow works.

| Detail | How we handle it |
| --- | --- |
| Templates need approval | Utility-style wording, no promotional language, submitted early |
| Consent | Orders imply a transactional context, but marketing messages need separate opt-in |
| Language | Hinglish or a regional language where your buyers use it |
| Pincode and address quality | Ask the buyer to confirm landmark and pincode in the chat |
| Mixed prepaid and COD | Only COD orders enter the flow; prepaid orders skip it |
| Festival peaks | Rules tuned for high order volume so replies are not buried |
| Provider costs | Per-conversation fees are separate and depend on your provider |

A confirmation message that reads like a promotion tends to be rejected or ignored. A short, plain message that lists the items, the amount payable at delivery and two buttons usually behaves better. Many stores also pair this with a prepaid nudge, offering a small incentive to pay online, which we can add if it suits your margins.

## Measuring it, and what comes after confirmation

The flow gives you four numbers worth watching: confirmation rate, cancellation rate, non-response rate and RTO on shipped COD orders. Tags on each order let you pull them from your store without a separate dashboard. Compare before and after across the same kind of weeks, because a sale period behaves differently from a quiet one.

### Optional additions

- **Failed-delivery follow-up.** A message after a non-delivery report asking the buyer to confirm availability or fix the address before return.
- **COD to prepaid nudge.** A payment link sent with a small incentive that you set, for buyers who would rather pay online.
- **Repeat-offender rules.** A tag for phone numbers or pincodes with several past refusals, so your team can hold or call before shipping. You decide the rule and review it, since rules that are too strict lose genuine orders.
- **Existing apps.** If you already use a COD verification app, we can connect to it, or move you to a cleaner setup. We tell you which is simpler.
