Most agencies we talk to are good at design, theme work and integrations, and quietly dislike catalog work. It is repetitive, it arrives late in the project, and the client's spreadsheet is never as clean as promised. This page describes how we take that work off your plate without your client ever feeling a handoff.

## What you get

- Product upload from client spreadsheets, supplier feeds or PDFs: titles, descriptions, variants, options, prices, SKUs, images matched to products, tags and collections.
- Migration data for replatforming projects: products, catalog-side data, redirect mapping and handle preservation.
- Catalog cleanup on a client's existing store: duplicate SKUs, inconsistent titles, missing alt text, broken variants.
- A QA sheet per project that lists what was built, what was queried and what was left out, formatted so you can forward it under your own name.
- A staging or draft state for your team to review before anything is published.

## How the handoff works

1. You send us the client's files and a short brief: platform, product count, anything the client is fussy about. Tell us the deadline you have already promised.
2. We inspect the files and send a fixed quote for the project. Nothing starts before you approve it.
3. The client adds a staff or collaborator account for us, or for your agency. We never take store passwords, and the account can be removed at any time.
4. Our AI engine builds the volume of the work and a person reviews every output. Products go in as drafts or into a staging store.
5. You review, send the query sheet to the client, and publish when you are happy. We fix anything you flag.

## Why agency work is different

Agency projects have constraints that a direct store owner does not.

| Agency concern | How we handle it |
| --- | --- |
| Client confidentiality | NDA on request, no client names in our shared files unless you add them |
| Your brand on deliverables | QA sheets and query lists can go out under your name |
| Access control | Client-owned collaborator account, removable at any time |
| Uneven project sizes | Same published starting prices for a 120-product job or a 900-product one |
| Late-arriving data | We flag gaps in a single query sheet rather than drip questions |
| Theme and app conflicts | We touch product data only, not theme code or app settings |

Platform detail matters too. On Shopify we respect the three-option limit and move extra attributes into metafields rather than forcing them into variants. On WooCommerce we build attributes and variations properly. On Magento and BigCommerce we handle configurables and option sets. Your developers do not have to explain any of this to us.

## Working in the client's store

Because we work through the client's own account, your client keeps ownership and visibility. They can see activity on their staff list, and you are never the person holding someone else's credentials. If your agreement with the client requires named individuals on the account, tell us at the start and we will set it up that way.

For larger replatforming jobs we usually agree a freeze window with you: a date after which the client stops editing the old catalog, so we do not build against a moving target. It is a small thing that prevents most late surprises.

## Scoping a project for your client

Quotes go faster, and are more accurate, when the first message includes a few facts. Send these and we can usually return a fixed quote after inspecting the files:

- **Product and variant count.** Variants drive the work more than product count does.
- **Source state.** Clean sheet, supplier PDFs, old-store export, or a mix.
- **Image state.** Are images named and matched to SKUs, or in loose folders?
- **Target platform and theme.** Which metafields or attributes the theme actually displays.
- **Extras.** Collections, SEO fields, redirects, alt text, or a monthly retainer for ongoing updates, from the Monthly Catalog Ops plan.

You set your own price to your client. We only need a clear scope and a date.

## Is this a fit?

It suits agencies and freelancers who regularly take on store builds or migrations and want catalog work handled reliably, without hiring a data-entry team. It also suits a one-off project where the catalog threatens the launch date.

It is probably not for you if the catalog is small and clean enough that your own team finishes it in an afternoon. In that case, do it yourself. And if a client expects us to design theme sections or build custom apps, that stays with your developers.
