Most UK stores moving to Shopify are coming off WooCommerce on a hosting plan that has started to creak, or off a Magento build that cost too much to maintain. The data transfers fine. The mistakes tend to come from tax, postage and the small things British shoppers notice at once: a price that suddenly shows ex-VAT, or a delivery promise that no longer matches.

## What you get

- **Products:** titles, descriptions, SKUs, prices, compare-at prices, weights in kilograms, images with alt text, tags and vendors, mapped to Shopify fields.
- **Variants and options:** sizes and colours rebuilt within Shopify's option limits, with a clear plan for products that need more.
- **Collections:** manual and automated collections recreated.
- **Customers and past orders:** brought in as records where your old platform exports them, set up so customers are not emailed during the import.
- **301 redirect map:** old product, category and page URLs matched to new ones, supplied as a file you can import.
- **VAT-ready data:** price format confirmed as gross or net, zero-rated and reduced-rate lines flagged.
- **A QA report:** before and after counts and a list of changes.

## What is specific to the UK

### Prices include VAT

UK shoppers expect to see the price they will pay. Shopify can show tax-inclusive prices for the UK market, but the setting is easy to miss and depends on your store address and tax registration. WooCommerce stores often hold prices net and add VAT with a plugin, so an import can quietly turn a £24.00 shirt into a £20.00 one, or into a £28.80 one. Before loading anything, we confirm with you which way the numbers are held, then test checkout with a UK postcode.

### Postage, couriers and delivery promises

Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and Parcelforce all price by weight and size band, so weights have to be right and in grams or kilograms consistently. We clean up weights during the migration and check that product pages do not promise delivery times the new shipping profiles will not meet. Northern Ireland and offshore postcodes often need their own rules, and we flag where your catalog data touches them.

### Spelling, units and product text

British spelling (colour, jewellery, trainers) matters for search as much as tone. Migrated descriptions written for a US supplier often say "sneakers" and "pants", which UK shoppers do not search for. We do not rewrite your whole catalog during a move unless you ask, but we flag the worst of it. Sizes are kept in UK sizing, with conversions in a size chart where you sell internationally.

### Marketplaces

If you list on Amazon.co.uk, eBay UK or OnBuy, we check that barcodes, brand and condition fields survive the move, since marketplace sync apps depend on them.

### Product safety and marking

Some categories, such as electricals, toys and cosmetics, carry labelling and marking requirements. We keep any existing safety or compliance text in product descriptions so it is not lost in the move. We do not give compliance advice.
