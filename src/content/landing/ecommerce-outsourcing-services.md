Outsourcing ecommerce operations has a bad reputation for good reasons: unclear scope, hourly billing, work that needs rewriting, and a stranger with the keys to your store. Done properly it is simpler than that. You decide what the business does. Someone else does the repetitive tasks around it, to a written spec, at a price you know in advance.

The pattern is consistent. These jobs produce an output you can inspect, they follow rules, and they scale with product count more than with judgement.

## What to keep in-house

- **Strategy:** which markets, which channels, what you stand for.
- **Buying and sourcing:** what to stock, supplier terms and margins.
- **Pricing and promotions:** they are business decisions with consequences.
- **Brand voice and creative direction:** we follow your guidelines, we do not write them.
- **Customer relationships that matter:** key accounts, complaints, partnerships.

If an outsourcer offers to own all of this, ask what they know about your customers that you do not.

## Productised, not hourly

| | Hourly outsourcing / BPO | Productised service |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing | Per hour or per seat | Fixed price per scope |
| Management | You brief and supervise the person | A written spec and a review before go-live |
| Quality | Depends on who is assigned | AI engine plus a person checking every output |
| Risk | Pay for slow or idle time | Price known before work starts |
| Start | Recruiting, onboarding, training | Send files, get a quote |

Hourly arrangements suit unpredictable, judgement-heavy work. Catalog and listing work is the opposite: predictable and checkable. Paying by the hour for it mostly rewards slowness. Our [guide to outsourcing product upload](/guides/outsource-product-upload) compares in-house, freelancers, apps and agencies in more detail, and [how to choose a listing agency](/how-to-choose-ecommerce-listing-agency) lists what to ask before you sign anything.

## How it works

1. You send a sample of the work: files, a catalog export, or a description of the problem.
2. We tell you what is in scope, what needs a decision from you, and quote a fixed price.
3. Our AI engine does the volume. A person reviews every output against your data and rules.
4. You approve on a draft, staging or preview state before anything is published.
5. For ongoing work, we move you onto a monthly plan with a defined monthly scope.

## Security and access

Outsourcing means someone touches your store, so be specific about how.

- We never ask for store passwords. We use a staff or collaborator account that you create and can remove.
- Permissions are limited to what the work needs. Catalog work does not need access to payment settings.
- Nothing goes live without your approval.
- Your data is used only for your project and never to train third-party models.
- You keep your exports and files. Leaving us means removing the account.

## Is this a fit?

It suits growing stores and small teams whose catalog, listings and channel setup have outgrown the time available, and agencies that want reliable delivery on client work. It suits founders who want to spend their hours on buying and brand.

It does not suit a store with a small, clean catalog that one person can manage in an afternoon a week. It does not suit someone looking for a full-time employee embedded in their team, or for a firm to run the business strategy. Be wary of anyone, including us, who says everything can be handed off.
