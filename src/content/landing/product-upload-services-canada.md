Selling in Canada means one catalogue serving two official languages, a patchwork of provincial taxes, and three or four marketplaces that each want the same product described differently. This page covers how we handle that work.

## What you get

- Products created on Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce or Magento from your files.
- Titles and descriptions in Canadian English, with French versions where you need them.
- Variants, options and SKUs built correctly, with weights in kilograms or grams.
- Prices uploaded in the currency you confirm, tagged taxable or exempt as you instruct.
- Barcodes (UPC and GTIN) checked for format and duplicates.
- Images matched to products with alt text, and a QA report listing anything missing.

## What is different about selling in Canada

### Provincial tax, handled by platform settings

Canada has GST nationally, HST in the provinces that combine it with provincial tax, and separate PST or QST elsewhere. Which applies depends on where the customer is and what you sell. That logic lives in the tax settings of Shopify or WooCommerce, not in the product rows. We do not set your rates or give tax advice. Our part is keeping product data clean enough for those settings to work: consistent taxable flags, correct product types and accurate weights. Confirm registration and rates with your accountant or the Canada Revenue Agency.

### English and French content

If you ship to Quebec, you will likely want a French version of the store. Quebec's Bill 96 strengthened French-language requirements for commercial activity, and the details depend on your business and products. We keep this general: we can produce French product content from your approved terminology, structure it so each language sits in its own field or translation app, and flag product names, size labels and care text that need a human decision. Whether your business must comply, and how, is a question for a lawyer.

Shopify was founded in Canada, and many Canadian merchants run Markets with a CAD store and a USD store. Shopify's three-options-per-product cap forces decisions for apparel, and WooCommerce needs attributes and variations built carefully.
