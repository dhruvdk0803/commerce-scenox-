Most catalog photo problems are not photography problems. The shot is fine, but one supplier sent a grey backdrop, another a warm cast, a third cropped the product off-centre, and the store now looks like five different shops. This service fixes that across the whole range, without a reshoot.

## What you get

- **Background removal to pure white:** clean cut-outs on true white (RGB 255, 255, 255) for marketplace mains, or transparent PNGs if you prefer.
- **Shadows:** a soft contact or drop shadow so products do not float, applied the same way across a collection.
- **Colour correction:** white balance and exposure adjusted so the product matches the real item, not a guess at a nicer one.
- **Cropping and centring:** product placed and scaled to your channels' specs, with consistent margins.
- **Retouching:** dust, lint, fingerprints, creases, stray threads and sensor spots removed.
- **Consistent sets:** every product in a collection edited to one look, so grids and feeds read as one range.
- **Naming and alt text:** SKU-based file names and alt text drafted from your product data.

This is the editing side of imagery. If you want new scenes generated from your photos, see [AI product photography](/ai-product-photography-service) or the tool-style [/ai-product-photos](/ai-product-photos).

## How it works

1. You send original files and a product sheet or export, plus the channels you sell on.
2. We inspect a sample, tell you what is fixable, and quote a fixed price per batch of 50 products.
3. Our AI engine handles the volume: cut-outs, exposure, alignment, cropping. A person then reviews every image against your source photo.
4. You approve a first batch. We adjust the style notes, then deliver the rest in the sizes and formats you need.
5. We can attach the finished images to products in your store through a staff or collaborator account you can remove. We never ask for store passwords.

## Keeping the true colour

Returns for "not as pictured" are almost always a colour or finish complaint. Editing tools make it easy to push saturation until a photo looks appealing, and that is the wrong goal. We work to the product.

| Problem in the source | What we do |
| --- | --- |
| Yellow or blue cast from room lighting | Neutralise white balance using a known-white or neutral area |
| Underexposed, muddy product | Lift exposure without clipping highlights |
| Reflective metal or glossy plastic | Keep reflections that show the material, remove only distracting ones |
| Dark fabrics losing texture | Open shadows so weave and stitching stay visible |
| No reference for a difficult colour | Flag it and ask you for a reference photo or swatch |

The rule is simple: if we cannot be confident a colour is right, we say so. A hundred accurate images beat a hundred attractive ones that come back as returns.

## Marketplace specs

Each channel has its own rules for background, minimum size and how much of the frame the product fills, and they change. We build to the current published requirements and note anything borderline. The [image requirements guide](/guides/product-image-requirements) lists the specifics for Shopify, Google, Amazon, Meta and Flipkart. A Merchant Center image rejection is a common reason people come to us; see our [Merchant Center fix service](/google-merchant-center-fix-service) for the feed side.

## Bulk work and consistency

Volume editing lives or dies on consistency. For a collection we agree three things up front: the canvas size and margin, the shadow style, and the crop rule per product type (a tall bottle and a flat wallet should not fill the frame identically). Once those are fixed, every image follows them, including products added later. For ongoing catalogs this pairs well with monthly [catalog operations](/ecommerce-catalog-management-services).

## Is this a fit?

It suits stores with 50 or more products, existing photos of reasonable quality, and a need for one consistent look across suppliers or shoots. It suits marketplace sellers who need compliant mains quickly.

It does not suit a brand that needs original editorial photography, or products where fine detail is the entire sale, such as bespoke jewellery. And a small, clean catalog of a dozen products is easy to do yourself with a free editor. If your photos are blurry or cropped too tightly, no editing recovers them, and we will say so before you pay.
