Store owners usually ask this question at one of three points: the catalog has outgrown its spreadsheet, a supplier has sent another messy file, or someone has suggested buying a PIM. The three options solve different problems, so the right choice depends on who does the work, how big the catalog is, and how many channels you feed.

## The three options in plain terms

A **catalog management service** is a team that does the data work: cleaning, enriching, uploading and maintaining listings. You pay per project or per month and receive finished product data on your platform.

**PIM software** (product information management) is a central database for product data with workflows, validation and channel exports. Akeneo, Plytix and Salsify are well-known examples. You license it, and your team operates it.

A **spreadsheet** is a CSV or Excel file you edit by hand and import into your platform. It is flexible and free, and it depends entirely on the discipline of whoever maintains it.

## What each one actually does

| | Catalog service | PIM software | Spreadsheets |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you get | Finished, uploaded listings | A system to manage data | A file |
| Who does the work | The service | Your team | Your team |
| Setup effort | Brief, files and access | Implementation, data modelling, integrations | None |
| Cost shape | Per project or monthly | Subscription, often plus implementation | Staff time only |
| Strength | Volume and clean-up without hiring | Governance, workflows, many channels | Simple and flexible |
| Weakness | Not a system of record on its own | Needs people and good data to feed it | Errors, versions, no validation |

The key difference is output. A PIM gives you a better place to manage data. It does not write your descriptions or fix your supplier files unless someone on your team does that work.

## Who each option suits

Size and team structure matter more than budget.

**Spreadsheets** suit up to a few hundred products, one editor and one sales channel. Keep a master file, use a consistent column template, and import in small batches.

**A catalog service** suits stores that have volume to process but not the staff to process it: a launch of 500 to several thousand listings, a migration, a supplier backlog, or a store that adds products in waves. The work is bursty, so paying per project is cheaper than hiring.

**PIM software** suits larger operations: thousands of products, several people editing, frequent supplier updates, and multiple channels such as your site, marketplaces and feeds, each with its own requirements. It also helps if you need approval steps and an audit trail.

## What is missing from each option

Each option has a gap that you should plan for.

A PIM does not remove the work of collecting, cleaning and enriching data. If you load it with inconsistent supplier files, you will have inconsistent data in a more expensive system.

A service does not give you ongoing governance by itself. If many people edit product data every day, you need a system that controls who can change what.

Spreadsheets have no validation, so a wrong column or an extra space in a variant name can break an import. We cover the common platform errors in [Shopify CSV import errors](/guides/shopify-csv-import-errors).

## When you need both

Many mid-size and larger stores end up with both. The PIM is the source of truth with workflows and exports. The service supplies labour: initial data cleanup, writing and enrichment, loading into the PIM, or keeping up with supplier updates.

A typical sequence is to clean and standardise the data first, then load it into the PIM, then keep the service on a monthly basis for the volume work. Doing these in reverse means paying for a system and then discovering the data is not ready for it.

## Decision table

Use this as a rough guide, not a rule.

| Your situation | Likely fit |
|---|---|
| Under 250 products, one editor, one platform | Spreadsheet, maybe a one-off cleanup |
| 250 to 1,000 products, no data person, launch or migration coming | Catalog service |
| Messy supplier files, new products added in waves | Catalog service, monthly if ongoing |
| 1,000+ products, several editors, multiple channels | PIM, possibly with a service for the data work |
| Strict approvals, audit trail, many suppliers | PIM |
| Enterprise-scale with an internal data team | PIM and an internal team |

Questions to ask yourself before buying anything: who will enter and maintain the data every week, how many channels need different versions of it, and how clean is the data you would load on day one.

## When to do it yourself and when to hand it off

If your catalog is small and clean, run it from a well-structured spreadsheet and your platform's own import. That is a fine setup, and not every store needs software or a service.

Hand it off when the volume or the mess exceeds your team's time: a large launch, a supplier backlog, or data that needs rewriting before any tool can use it.
