This is the most serious category of issue, because it affects the whole account and not one product. It is also the least mechanical: there is no single field to correct. This guide covers what Google looks at, how to audit your site and how to request a review. For other disapprovals, see the [Merchant Center disapprovals guide](/guides/google-merchant-center-disapprovals).

## How the issue appears

An account-level notice appears at the top of Merchant Center and under the setup and policy issues area, naming the violation and listing a deadline if it is a warning. Google's [misrepresentation policy page](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150127) defines it as deceiving users by excluding relevant product information or providing misleading information about products and businesses.

## What triggers it

From Google's policy page, listed violations include:

- A false identity, business name or contact information, or hiding your real business location or product source.
- Impersonating a brand or business.
- Denying returns or refunds when your own policy allows them.
- Failing to clearly disclose the payment model and full costs: total price with taxes and fees, recurring costs, billing intervals and shipping, including return shipping.
- Data that does not match the site: prices, availability or attributes.
- Unreliable claims or manipulated media.

Google may look at your listings, website, accounts and third-party sources.

## How to audit your site

1. **Who you are.** Is there a real business name, physical or registered address where appropriate, phone number and email? Do they match Merchant Center settings?
2. **Policies.** Are returns, refunds, shipping and privacy terms easy to find and consistent with how you actually operate?
3. **Pricing clarity.** Does the page show the total cost, including taxes and fees where relevant, before checkout?
4. **Checkout.** Can a customer complete an order? Is the payment model clear?
5. **Product accuracy.** Do titles, images, prices and stock match what the customer receives?
6. **Reviews and claims.** Remove claims you cannot substantiate.
7. **Data match.** Compare feed values to pages for a sample of products.

## Fix steps for Shopify

1. Add or update the legal and policy pages in settings, including refund, shipping, privacy and terms, and link them in the footer.
2. Put contact details on a visible contact page and in the footer.
3. Check the store's checkout shows taxes and shipping before payment.
4. Make sure the Google & YouTube app's synced data matches the storefront. See the [price mismatch guide](/guides/merchant-center-price-mismatch) for the common divergence.
5. Remove products or claims that violate the policy.

## Fix steps for other platforms and feeds

- Publish the same policy and contact content on your site regardless of platform, and keep it in the footer of every page.
- Make sure feed data comes from live store data, not static exports.
- Remove violating offers from your data source before requesting a review. Google's process says bulk appeals require removing all violating offers first.
- Keep records: screenshots of policies and checkout may help if you appeal.

## How to request a review

Google's page says to fix the website and remove violating products, update the product data in Merchant Center or wait for the scheduled upload, then request a review or appeal. Edited products are re-reviewed automatically and removed products need no request. If you believe the decision is wrong, you can submit a standard appeal with a reason and any required documents. Account reviews typically take about seven business days.

## Prevention

- Treat website transparency as part of the catalog: policies, contact and checkout clarity.
- Keep Merchant Center business details current.
- Keep feeds fresh so the site and data never disagree.
- Review the policy page when you change business model, add subscriptions or add a new market.
