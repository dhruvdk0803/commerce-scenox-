Most product titles are written once, in a hurry, by whoever was uploading that day. Then they are stuck with them for years. A title is the line that appears in Google results, in Shopping ads, in marketplace search and at the top of your own product page, so a few minutes of structure here pays back everywhere.

This guide gives you formulas by category, the length limits that matter, the things to strip out, and before and after examples you can copy.

## The short version of a good title

A good title answers one question: what exactly is this? It uses the words a shopper would type, in the order they would type them, and adds only the attributes that separate this product from the next one.

The working pattern is:

```
[Brand] + [Product type] + [Key attribute(s)] + [Variant detail]
```

Everything else is a variation on that. Which part comes first depends on how people search for the product, which is the next section.

## Decide what shoppers actually type

Before writing a formula, look at how your buyers search. There are two broad cases.

**Brand-led searches.** People searching "Nike Air Max 90 white" or "Philips air fryer 4.1L" already know the brand. Put it first. These shoppers are comparing and the brand is part of the product name.

**Type-led searches.** People searching "women's linen shirt" or "stainless steel water bottle 1 litre" have no brand in mind. If your brand is not known yet, a title that starts with the brand wastes the strongest position. Lead with the product type and attributes, then the brand.

A quick test: type your product into Google as a stranger would. See what the top results call it, and which words appear in the titles of products that are not yours. That is your vocabulary.

## Title formulas by category

These are patterns, not rules. Keep the order the same across a category so the catalog reads consistently.

| Category | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apparel | Brand + Gender + Type + Material/Fit + Colour | Fabindia Women's Cotton Kurta, Straight Fit, Indigo |
| Footwear | Brand + Gender + Type + Model + Colour | Bata Men's Leather Formal Shoes, Derby, Black |
| Electronics | Brand + Model + Type + Key spec | Samsung Galaxy Buds2 Wireless Earbuds, Noise Cancelling |
| Home and kitchen | Brand + Type + Capacity/Size + Material | Prestige Pressure Cooker 5 Litre, Stainless Steel |
| Beauty | Brand + Line + Type + Size + Variant | Lakme 9to5 Primer Matte Lipstick, 3.6 g, Rose Dust |
| Furniture | Brand + Type + Dimensions + Material + Colour | Oak Writing Desk, 120 x 60 cm, Solid Wood, Natural |
| Food and grocery | Brand + Product + Variety + Pack size | Tata Tea Gold, Assam Blend, 500 g |

Two notes. First, put the attribute buyers filter on most in the early positions: size for beds, capacity for appliances, material for jewellery. Second, size and colour belong in the title only when each variant is a separate listing or feed item. On a Shopify product with variants, the title usually stays variant-free and the feed adds colour and size for each variant.

## Length limits that matter

Different surfaces cut titles at different points.

| Surface | Practical limit | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page title in Google search | About 60 to 70 characters displayed (pixel-based, not fixed) | Front-load the important words |
| Google Merchant Center feed title | 150 characters allowed | Put the key words in the first 70 |
| Shopify product title | 255 characters allowed | Shorter is better; keep the product page readable |
| Amazon | Category dependent; often up to 200 characters, with some categories lower | Check the style guide for your category in Seller Central |

Check the current limits in Google's [product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112) and the Seller Central style guide for your category before building a template around any number here. Limits change, and Google Shopping shows far fewer characters than it accepts.

The rule that never changes is to put what matters first. If a title is truncated after 70 characters, the part that is cut should be the least useful part.

## What to leave out

Most title problems come from adding things, not missing them.

- **Promotional text.** "Sale", "Free Shipping", "Best Price", "Buy 1 Get 1". Google Merchant Center disapproves these and marketplaces suppress listings for them.
- **All capitals and decorative symbols.** "BEST LEATHER WALLET ***" reads as spam to people and to feed validators.
- **Keyword stuffing.** "Wallet Men Wallet Leather Wallet Gents Purse" is not a title. One mention of each important word is enough.
- **Internal codes.** Supplier SKUs, season codes and warehouse tags belong in their own fields.
- **Repeated brand.** The brand once, not at both ends.
- **Information that varies by variant, when variants share one listing.** Putting "Blue, Size M" in the title of a product that has twelve variants is wrong for eleven of them.

## Worked examples: before and after

Here are four real-looking titles in the shape we see most often in supplier files, with a fix for each.

**Example 1: apparel**

Before:

```
NEW ARRIVAL Cotton Shirt Mens Full Sleeve Casual SALE
```

After:

```
Raymond Men's Cotton Casual Shirt, Full Sleeve, Slim Fit, Navy
```

What changed: promotional words removed, brand added, fit added because shoppers filter by it, colour moved to the end where it can vary by variant in the feed.

**Example 2: electronics**

Before:

```
Headphones BT 5.0 black
```

After:

```
Sony WH-CH520 Wireless Bluetooth Headphones, On-Ear, 50 Hour Battery, Black
```

What changed: model number added, because people search by model. A key spec (battery life) added. The shortcode "BT" written out.

**Example 3: home goods**

Before:

```
Steel bottle
```

After:

```
Milton Thermosteel Water Bottle 1 Litre, Stainless Steel, Vacuum Insulated
```

What changed: brand, capacity and material added. Capacity is the attribute most buyers use to compare bottles, so it sits right after the type.

**Example 4: own-label product**

Before:

```
Aarav Home Collection Cushion Cover Set Floral Print Luxury Premium Quality
```

After:

```
Floral Cushion Covers, Set of 5, 16 x 16 Inch, Cotton Canvas, Aarav Home
```

What changed: for an unknown brand the product type leads. "Luxury" and "Premium Quality" are claims with no search value and were removed. Set size and dimensions added.

## Titles in your CSV and your feed

Titles live in more than one place. On Shopify the column is `Title` (the product title) and the search-engine title is a separate field, `SEO Title`. If you leave `SEO Title` empty, Shopify uses the product title. For a catalog of any size, it is worth setting both: the product title for readability, the SEO title for the search snippet.

For Google Shopping, the feed title is separate again. Apps often pass the Shopify title through unchanged, which is fine when the title is clean and a problem when it is not. If the same product needs a longer or more descriptive title in the feed than on the page, build it with a rule: for example, `brand + title + colour + size`. Our guide to [Google Merchant Center disapprovals](/guides/google-merchant-center-disapprovals) covers what the validator rejects.

## How to retitle a catalog without breaking things

A bulk title rewrite is easy to do badly. A sequence that works:

1. Export the catalog and sort by revenue or traffic. Do not retitle everything blindly.
2. Check Google Search Console for which products already get impressions under their current titles. Those are the ones to change with care.
3. Write the formula for each category and test it on ten products.
4. Apply it to the rest in a spreadsheet, using concatenation formulas on clean attribute columns (see [product attributes and taxonomy](/guides/product-attributes-taxonomy)).
5. Keep the URL handle unchanged. Changing a title does not change the handle on re-import unless you change the `Handle` column, and handle changes need redirects.
6. Review a sample by eye. Formulas produce odd results when an attribute is missing, such as "Cotton Shirt, , Navy".

The last step is the one people skip. Look at the rows where a field was blank.

## A copy-paste title audit

Run these checks on any ten products:

- Does the title say what the product is in the first five words?
- Is the brand in the right place for how buyers search?
- Is there at least one attribute that separates it from similar products?
- Is it free of sale text, symbols and shouting?
- Is the same pattern used across the category?
- Are the first 70 characters enough on their own?

Three or more fails on a category means the formula is the problem, not the individual products.
