Most weak product pages share one problem: the description says nothing a shopper can use. "Premium quality, perfect for every occasion" tells nobody the fabric, the fit or whether it works with their phone. The templates below fix that by forcing every description to carry the facts people compare before buying.

Each category has a fill-in template and one worked example. All example products are invented samples, not real items.

## What every good product description contains

A good description answers five questions in this order: what is it, who is it for, what are the specifications that matter, how is it used or cared for, and what does the buyer receive. Put the answer to the first question in the first sentence, because that is the text search results and AI answers tend to pull.

Keep paragraphs short. Put comparable facts (size, material, capacity, ingredients) in a list or table so shoppers can scan, and keep persuasion to one or two sentences. If a sentence could be pasted onto any other product, cut it.

## Title formulas that hold up across a catalog

Use one formula per category and apply it to every product. The general pattern is:

```
Brand + Product type + Key attribute + Variant (colour / size / pack)
```

Examples of the pattern, using invented brands:

- `Northline Linen Shirt, Slim Fit, Sky Blue, M`
- `Aurel 65W USB-C Charger, 2 Ports, White`
- `Verdane Vitamin C Serum, 30 ml`
- `Haldor Solid Oak Bookcase, 5 Shelf, Natural`

Titles show a lot of information in a small space, so put the most searched words first. Skip promotional wording such as "best" or "sale", and avoid ALL CAPS. Google Merchant Center has its own title requirements, so check [Merchant Center's product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants) when you prepare a feed.

## Templates by category

### Fashion

Buyers need fit, fabric and care before anything else. Returns in this category are mostly about size and expectation, so precision here saves money.

```
[Product type] in [fabric and composition]. [One sentence on fit and cut: slim, relaxed, cropped, model height and size worn].
Details: [neckline/closure/pockets/lining].
Care: [wash and iron instructions].
Size and fit: [measurements or size chart link].
```

Filled sample (invented product):

> The Northline Linen Shirt is a slim-fit shirt in 100% linen with a soft, lived-in texture. It sits close through the chest and shoulders; the model is 183 cm and wears size M.
>
> Details: button-through front, single chest pocket, curved hem.
> Care: machine wash cold, line dry, iron on a medium setting.
> Size and fit: chest 98 cm in size M; see the size chart for the full range.

### Electronics

Shoppers compare specifications, so lead with the product type and the three specs that decide the purchase. Always list what is in the box and what is compatible.

```
[Product type] with [headline spec 1], [headline spec 2] and [headline spec 3]. [One sentence on the main use].
Specifications: [model number, dimensions, weight, ports/connectivity, power].
Compatible with: [devices or standards].
In the box: [items]. Warranty: [period and terms].
```

Filled sample (invented product):

> The Aurel 65W Charger is a two-port USB-C wall charger that can power a laptop and a phone at the same time.
>
> Specifications: 65 W maximum output, 2 x USB-C, foldable plug, 82 g.
> Compatible with: USB Power Delivery devices including most USB-C laptops, tablets and phones.
> In the box: charger and quick-start card. Warranty: 12 months.

### Beauty and skincare

Name the skin or hair concern, the key actives with concentrations if you have them, and the full ingredient list. Avoid medical claims you cannot support, and check the rules for cosmetic claims in the markets you sell to.

```
[Product type] formulated with [key active(s)] for [skin/hair type or concern].
How to use: [steps, frequency, time of day].
Key ingredients: [actives and what they do, in plain words].
Full ingredients: [INCI list]. Size: [ml / g]. Free from: [only what is true].
```

Filled sample (invented product):

> The Verdane Vitamin C Serum is a lightweight daily serum with 10% ascorbic acid for dull, uneven-looking skin.
>
> How to use: apply 3 to 4 drops to clean skin in the morning, then moisturiser and sunscreen.
> Key ingredients: ascorbic acid (antioxidant), hyaluronic acid (hydration).
> Full ingredients: listed in the pack information. Size: 30 ml. Patch test before first use.

### Home and furniture

Dimensions and assembly are the top sources of returns and questions. Give them exact figures, and state materials honestly (solid wood and veneer are not the same).

```
[Product type] in [material and finish], sized for [use or space].
Dimensions: [W x D x H], [weight], [load capacity].
Materials: [frame, surface, hardware].
Assembly: [required or not, time, tools]. Care: [cleaning].
```

Filled sample (invented product):

> The Haldor Bookcase is a five-shelf bookcase in solid oak with a natural oil finish, sized to fit an alcove or a wall.
>
> Dimensions: 80 W x 30 D x 180 H cm, 28 kg, 15 kg per shelf.
> Materials: solid oak frame and shelves, steel fixings.
> Assembly: required, about 40 minutes, tools included. Care: wipe with a dry cloth.

### Jewellery

Buyers want the metal, stone, size and certification. Be exact about plating versus solid metal, and give dimensions in millimetres.

```
[Product type] in [metal and purity / plating], set with [stone, cut, size].
Dimensions: [length / diameter / drop], [weight].
Finish and clasp: [details]. Certification: [hallmark / lab report if any].
Care: [storage, cleaning, what to avoid].
```

Filled sample (invented product):

> The Lysa Drop Earrings are handmade drop earrings in 925 sterling silver with a freshwater pearl.
>
> Dimensions: 32 mm drop, pearl about 7 mm, 3.2 g per pair.
> Finish and clasp: polished silver, butterfly backs. Hallmark: 925.
> Care: store dry in the pouch provided; remove before swimming or bathing.

### Food and grocery

Allergens, ingredients, weight, shelf life and storage are not optional, and labelling rules vary by country. Take them from the pack, not from memory.

```
[Product type], [flavour/variety], [weight or volume].
Ingredients: [full list in order]. Allergens: [as printed on pack].
Nutrition: [per 100 g or per serving, as on pack].
Storage: [instructions]. Shelf life: [period]. Origin: [country/region].
```

Filled sample (invented product):

> Harlow Mills Rolled Oats, 1 kg, are whole-grain rolled oats for porridge, baking and granola.
>
> Ingredients: 100% whole-grain oats. Allergens: may contain traces of other cereals, as printed on pack.
> Storage: keep sealed in a cool, dry place. Shelf life: 12 months from packing.

## Length guidance

There is no ranking-safe word count, so write what the buyer needs. As a working range, use 80 to 200 words of description plus a specification list for most products. Commodity items such as a plain cable can run shorter. Complex products such as skincare or electronics can run longer, with the detail in structured sections rather than one long block.

Look at what your returns and pre-sale questions are about and add those answers to the description. That is a better length rule than a number.

## Writing descriptions at scale from supplier data

The reliable way to write 500 descriptions is to treat them as data first and prose second. Supplier files usually arrive with abbreviated attributes, mixed units and inconsistent capitalisation, so clean the columns before you write anything.

1. Standardise attributes into columns: material, size, colour, capacity, care, warranty.
2. Fix units (cm or inches, ml or fl oz) per target market.
3. Write one template per category, as above, with fields mapped to those columns.
4. Draft from the template, then vary the opening sentence by the product's strongest feature.
5. Review a sample from every batch by hand, and check every number against the source.

## Using AI drafts with human review

AI is good at turning structured attributes into readable first drafts, and bad at knowing when a claim is false. Google's published guidance says generative AI can help create content, but using it to mass-produce pages with no added value can breach its spam policy on scaled content abuse. It also advises checking AI output for accuracy before publishing. See [Google's guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content).

For merchants, the same page notes that Merchant Center expects AI-generated product data, such as titles and descriptions, to be labelled as AI-generated in the feed. Read the current policy before you set up a feed.

In practice, that means a human checks specifications, sizes, ingredients and claims, removes anything that cannot be verified, and rewrites repeated phrasing. The goal is helpful pages, however they were drafted.

## Category and collection content

Category pages need a short intro (two or three sentences) that says what the collection contains and how to choose. Add a few lines of buying guidance below the product grid if it helps, such as sizing advice or how to compare options. Keep it useful rather than keyword-stuffed, and link to related collections and your [pricing](/pricing) or policy pages only where a shopper would want them.
