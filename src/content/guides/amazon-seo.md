Most Amazon listings lose before the shopper reaches them. The title is vague, the category is slightly off, the backend field is stuffed with words Amazon ignores, and the second image is a copy of the first. This guide covers what you control, in the order we would fix it.

One caveat first. Amazon does not publish its ranking formula, and nobody outside Amazon knows the weights. Where a claim below is a rule, it comes from Amazon's own seller documentation. Where it is experience, it says so.

## How Amazon search matches a listing

Amazon search does two jobs. It finds products that match the words typed, then orders them by how likely they are to satisfy the shopper. The first job is text matching. If your listing never contains the words someone searches, it will not appear, however good your product is.

The second job is where Amazon's descriptions stay general. Amazon says shopper behaviour and product performance matter: whether people click, buy and are satisfied. That is why Amazon SEO and conversion are the same work. A keyword-stuffed title that nobody clicks does worse than a clear one that sells.

## The title

The title is the most heavily read field, and the one most likely to be wrong. Amazon sets title rules per category in its style guides. Limits are commonly up to 200 characters, lower in some categories, and Amazon asks for no promotional phrases, no repeated words and no decorative symbols. Check the current rule for your category and marketplace before writing.

A formula that works for most categories:

```
Brand + Product type + Key attribute + Size or quantity + Colour or model
```

An example for an invented brand:

```
Ridgeway Insulated Steel Water Bottle, 750 ml, Leakproof Lid, Matte Black
```

Put the words a shopper types first. Mobile results cut titles short, so the first 60 or so characters need to carry the sale. Do not copy a long title because a competitor has one. See [product title formulas](/guides/product-title-seo) for more patterns.

## Bullet points

Amazon displays five bullets prominently. Use them for the questions a shopper asks before buying: what it is made from, what it fits, what size, what is in the box, what it does.

- Start each bullet with the feature in a few words, then the detail.
- One idea per bullet. Do not pack three claims into one line.
- Use real numbers and materials from your spec sheet.
- Do not make claims you cannot support: health claims, "best seller", or comparisons with named brands.

Keyword use should read naturally. If a bullet sounds like a search query, rewrite it. Bullets are also a good place to answer the questions that appear in your reviews and Q&A.

## Backend search terms

Seller Central has a hidden "search terms" field, called generic keywords in some templates. Amazon limits it: 250 bytes on most marketplaces, and a lower limit applies on Amazon.in. Check the field's help text for your marketplace. Amazon's guidance, as published for sellers, is to avoid repeating what is already in visible fields, to leave out brand names and other identifiers, and to avoid terms that do not describe the product.

Use the field for what the visible copy cannot hold: synonyms, spelling variants, alternate names, common misspellings that are not offensive, and use cases. Space-separate the words. Commas and repeats waste bytes. If you go over the limit, expect the field to be ignored or rejected, so leave a margin. Accented characters can cost more than one byte each.

## Description and A+ Content

The description is secondary for search, but it still matters for conversion. Brand-registered sellers can add A+ Content: modules with images, comparison charts and brand story. Treat it as a conversion tool. Use it to show size comparisons, what is included, and how the product is used. Amazon reviews A+ Content before it goes live, and it has its own content rules, so read them before you commission anything.

## Images

Main image: product alone on a plain white background, filling most of the frame, with no text, logos or badges. Amazon's image rules are specific and category-dependent, so check the current requirements. Then use the other slots deliberately:

1. A close-up of the feature people ask about.
2. The product in use, with scale.
3. A dimension or size graphic.
4. What is in the box.
5. A comparison or detail shot.

Our [image requirements guide](/guides/product-image-requirements) lists the specs by platform. Poor images cost sales in a way that no keyword can fix.

## Reviews, sales and other signals

Amazon acknowledges in general terms that sales performance, customer satisfaction and availability affect how products are shown. It does not disclose the weighting, and we will not invent one. What you can control:

- Stay in stock. A suppressed or unavailable listing earns nothing.
- Price competitively against comparable products.
- Fulfil reliably, whether by Amazon or yourself.
- Respond to what reviews say: fix the product or the listing copy that creates the complaint.
- Never buy, swap or incentivise reviews. Amazon prohibits it and enforces it.

## Listing optimisation checklist

1. Category and browse node match the product, not the supplier's label.
2. Title follows the category style guide and leads with brand, product type and key attribute.
3. Five bullets, one idea each, facts from the spec sheet.
4. Description or A+ Content answers the remaining questions.
5. Backend search terms within the byte limit, no repeats, no brand names.
6. Required attributes filled, including those that only appear in filters.
7. Barcodes valid, or a GTIN exemption in place where eligible.
8. Variations grouped under one parent with the right theme.
9. Main image compliant; at least five more images in use.
10. Price, stock and shipping settings competitive and accurate.

## Worked example: before and after

Here is a typical supplier-sourced listing and how we would reshape it. The product and brand are invented.

Before:

```
Title: RIDGEWAY BOTTLE BEST QUALITY!!! Free Gift
Bullet 1: Great bottle for everyone
Bullet 2: Keeps drinks hot and cold
Search terms: ridgeway, bottle, water bottle, water bottle steel, bottle for water
```

After:

```
Title: Ridgeway Insulated Steel Water Bottle, 750 ml, Leakproof Lid, Matte Black
Bullet 1: DOUBLE-WALL INSULATION: 18/8 stainless steel body, vacuum sealed between the walls
Bullet 2: LEAKPROOF LID: screw-top with silicone seal, tested upside down at the factory
Search terms: flask thermos sports gym hiking commuter cold drinks hot drinks tumbler
```

The rewritten title drops the promotional words Amazon's style guides discourage and leads with what the product is. The bullets state a feature and a fact. The search terms stop repeating the title and add synonyms a shopper might type. (Only write "tested" if you have the test record.)

## Category and attributes

Category choice sits upstream of everything else. The browse node decides which attributes Amazon asks for, which filters your product appears in and which style guide applies. A product in a near-miss category can pass upload and still lose, because its size or material never reaches the filter panel.

Fill every attribute the category offers that you have data for, not just the required ones. Colour, size, material, pack count and compatibility feed the left-hand filters that many shoppers use. Standardise values: "Black", not "blk" in one row and "Jet Black" in another. A spreadsheet pass for consistency is dull and pays for itself.

## Variations

Variations group sizes, colours or pack counts under one parent so reviews and traffic pool on one page. Choose the variation theme the category allows, and give every child the attributes that theme needs. Do not merge products that are not the same item in different sizes or colours. Amazon polices variation abuse and it can cost you listings. If your catalogue spans hundreds of parent and child rows, build and check it in a flat file; our [flat file guide](/guides/amazon-flat-file-listing) shows how.

## Measuring what you changed

Change one group of things at a time and write down the date. Amazon's Seller Central reports show traffic and conversion at the listing level, and Brand Registry sellers get additional search and brand analytics. Compare a few weeks before and after, allowing for season and promotions. If you change title, images and price the same day, you will not know which one worked.

Do not chase rank for one keyword. A listing that appears for forty relevant searches and converts is worth more than one that holds position three for a single phrase.
